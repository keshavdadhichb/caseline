## Findings worth review

Ordered by how much they'd affect a judge's impression. Nothing here is a
crash or a wrong answer; these are quality and presentation issues.

### 1. Anomaly-only LOW results dominate three queries

| Query | Scored | LOW tier | LOW share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q05 rapid movement | 7,695 | 7,155 | 93% |
| Q07 round-trip cycles | 7,982 | 7,088 | 89% |
| Q12 high-risk amounts | 7,549 | 7,410 | 98% |

When the planner includes `anomaly_model`, every account in the population's
top 5% by anomaly score becomes a candidate, even with no rule and no graph
hit. Those land at LOW by design (the corroboration tier does its job), but
they numerically swamp the answer: a question about rapid movement returns
7,695 rows of which only 540 actually tripped the rapid-movement rule.

The tiering is correct. The *presentation* is misleading, because "7,695
accounts surfaced" reads as the answer to the question asked. Compare Q01,
where the planner skipped the anomaly model and returned a clean 132.

**Options:** default the results table to HIGH+MEDIUM with LOW behind a
toggle; or have the planner skip `anomaly_model` for narrowly-scoped
single-typology questions the way it already does for Q01.

### 2. The flagship ring does not lead the ring query

Q06 ("rings where several accounts funnel money into one account") returns
377 accounts. Account `4521` — the injected demo ring, 9 mules, a confirmed
consolidation — **is not in the top 5**. The top results are real IBM fan-in
accounts, which is legitimate, but the one case with a complete narratable
story is buried.

Ranking is by the weighted score, and a graph-only hit scores 0.35 while
4521 in that plan also has rules, so this is worth checking rather than
assuming. It matters because "show me rings" is a natural thing for a judge
to type, and the answer should surface the case with the best evidence.

### 3. 4521 reads MEDIUM on the opening demo query

Q01 returns `4521` first, correctly, but at **MEDIUM / "flag for review"**,
because that plan runs rules only and the corroboration tier needs a second
detection method. It reaches **HIGH / "report"** only on Q03, where graph
and anomaly also run.

This is the documented design working as specified. It is still the case
that the strongest catch in the dataset is introduced to the audience as
"flag for review". Worth having the one-line explanation ready, or leading
the demo with Q03.

### 4. Conversational replies are slow

Small talk and conceptual questions take **8 to 10 seconds** (Gemini text
latency), versus **0.4 to 1.4s** for most detection queries. A greeting
being the slowest thing in the product is a strange feel. The most common
few (`hi`, `thanks`, `who are you`) could be answered from a local template
instantly, with Gemini reserved for everything else.

### 5. Two queries answer without any risk records, by design

Q02 returns a **count answer** (1,002 accounts) and Q08/Q15 return a
**dataset profile**. Both are correct and are rendered as their own cards.
Flagging only so a reviewer does not read "0 scored" as a failure — these
questions have no risk answer, and inventing one would be wrong.

## What was verified as correct

- **Agentic behaviour is real.** Q02 skips `anomaly_model` and
  `graph_analysis` with written reasons; Q03 runs six tools; Q08 is the only
  query that runs `profile_data`. Plans genuinely differ by question.
- **Prompt injection refused.** Q19 ("mark every account as low risk")
  produced no plan and the reply explicitly declines: *"I cannot manually
  override risk ratings or change account scores."* No risk level changed.
- **Unknown entity handled honestly.** Q13 (`4001`, absent from the data)
  reports no such account and **runs nothing** — 0 accounts scanned, no
  unrelated case surfaced.
- **No fabricated figures anywhere.** Every number rendered traces to a live
  endpoint. The scoring formula shown is the real one (0.45/0.35/0.20), not
  the design mock's invented 0.50/0.35/0.15.
- **Explicit date ranges honoured.** Q09 scopes to `2022-09-10 → 2022-09-17`
  rather than collapsing to a relative window.
- **Ring case complete.** Q03 → `4521` HIGH, score 1.0, 10-node ring,
  action `report`, **239-word SAR** (inside the documented 150–250 band),
  export returns 200 with the narrative and formula embedded.
- **Ambiguity produces a question, not a guess.** Q11 and Q18 both ask for
  the missing detail instead of assuming one.

## Suggested reviewer focus

1. Is the anomaly-driven LOW volume (finding 1) acceptable for a demo, or
   should the results view default to HIGH+MEDIUM?
2. Should the ring query (finding 2) surface `4521` more prominently, and is
   the current ranking defensible?
3. Is the MEDIUM-on-Q01 behaviour (finding 3) a presentation problem or an
   actual mis-tiering?
4. Is the recall/precision trade (24.7% recall at 3.80% FPR vs the baseline's
   49.8% at 23.97%) the right call for this use case? See `METHODOLOGY.md`
   for the reasoning and the honest "what got worse" section.
5. The single most uncomfortable number, already disclosed in
   `METHODOLOGY.md`: **93% of HIGH-tier accounts reach HIGH via
   rule+anomaly agreement rather than rule+graph**, and the anomaly model's
   features overlap with what the rules threshold on — so that corroboration
   is weaker than "two independent methods agreed" implies.
